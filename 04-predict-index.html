BEFORE THE CHECKPOINT

Say what you expect

You have just read how compensation fails and why some findings read as improvement when they are the opposite. Before you answer anything, commit to a prediction. It takes about five minutes.

THE STRATEGY

Predict before you check

Say what you expect to find before you look. Then look. The gap between your prediction and the answer is specific, and it points at the reasoning that produced it rather than at a topic.

Reading an answer you never predicted teaches almost nothing, because you cannot tell whether you would have got there. Committing first makes the comparison mean something.

IF THIS IS RECOVERY
  • Blood pressure steady or rising
  • Skin warming, colour returning
  • Urine output rising above 30 mL/hr
  • Mental status unchanged, fully alert

Everything moves the same way at once. The heart rate falls because the demand on it has fallen, so the findings that reflect perfusion improve alongside it.

IF COMPENSATION IS FAILING
  • Blood pressure falling
  • Skin cool, pale, mottled, refill slowing
  • Urine output falling further, toward none
  • Mental status declining, drowsy or confused

The heart rate falls because the response producing it can no longer be sustained. Nothing else improves, because nothing was fixed.

The same number, two opposite meanings. A heart rate of 94 is not the finding. The direction of everything around it is the finding, and mental status is the earliest of those to move.

    Why this works

    A prediction makes your reasoning visible before the answer can contaminate it. Once you have read the correct answer, you cannot tell whether you would have produced it, because it now looks obvious. Writing the prediction first removes that problem permanently.

    It also changes what the answer teaches you. Instead of confirming something, it corrects something specific, and a specific correction is easier to act on than a general sense that you need to review.

    Carry it forward

    Before you read the answer to any practice question, say what you expect and why. Before you turn a page that reveals an outcome, say what you expect to find there. When your prediction and the answer disagree, the reason for the disagreement is the thing to study.

    Now take the checkpoint. It gives you a client whose heart rate has fallen, and asks what it means. You have just built the reasoning for that. Commit to an answer before you look at the feedback.
